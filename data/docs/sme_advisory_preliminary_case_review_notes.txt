SME ADVISORY – PRELIMINARY CASE REVIEW NOTES
Fictional internal sample material for demonstration use only.
This file is synthetic and does not describe any real institution, customer, product, or policy.

Document owner: SME Advisory Support
Document type: Internal working note
Review cycle: Quarterly
Audience: Relationship managers and advisory specialists

1. Purpose
This note supports early-stage review of small and medium-sized business customer cases.
It is intended to help staff form a consistent preliminary view before a case is escalated for deeper review.
It is not an approval document and it must not be treated as a final credit, risk, or product decision.

2. Typical use of this note
This note may be used when a relationship manager needs to:
- compare a customer situation against basic product purpose
- identify obvious gaps in the available information
- recognise whether a case appears straightforward, borderline, or incomplete
- explain internally why a case may need escalation

3. Product purpose reminders
FlexLine Demo is generally discussed in short-term working capital situations.
Typical examples include seasonal inventory pressure, supplier prepayments, or timing gaps between outgoing costs and incoming customer payments.

InvoiceBridge Demo is generally discussed when the customer has a visible business-to-business invoicing pattern and a financing need linked to receivables timing.

AssetGrow Demo is generally discussed when the customer has an identifiable equipment, machinery, or operational investment need.

4. Preliminary fit observations
A preliminary fit view should remain cautious and evidence-based.
Even when a case looks commercially attractive, staff should avoid positive wording if key supporting facts are missing.

Indicators that usually support a more positive preliminary view include:
- sufficient operating history
- latest financial statements available
- no unresolved tax arrears visible in the sample data
- business profile that matches product purpose
- balance sheet and profitability indicators that do not immediately raise concern

Indicators that usually weaken support include:
- repeated payment delays
- weak equity ratio
- unusually high leverage relative to EBITDA
- missing financial statements
- product request that does not clearly match the stated business need

5. Incomplete-information handling
If key facts are missing, the case should not be described as clearly aligned.
Instead, the note should identify what is missing.

Examples of missing information that commonly limit a preliminary view:
- latest financial statements
- confirmed financing purpose
- current debt detail
- invoicing mix information for receivables-based discussion
- evidence about concentration risk

Recommended wording:
The available internal sample material does not provide enough information for a confident preliminary view. Additional customer or case information is required.

6. Escalation situations
Escalation should be considered when:
- repeated payment delays are present
- unresolved tax arrears are visible
- leverage appears elevated
- one customer appears to represent a large share of revenue
- profitability is weak relative to the requested use case
- important case facts are missing or inconsistent

8. Recording guidance
Internal notes should separate:
- facts observed from structured customer data
- statements supported by internal product or policy documents
- assumptions or unknowns that still require clarification
